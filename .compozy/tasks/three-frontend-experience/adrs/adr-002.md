# ADR-002: Use a Permissioned Role-Adaptive Dashboard for Internal and Customer Users

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-07

## Context

Managers and employees need to operate inspections and prioritize review work. Viewers, clients, and property owners need to follow only the assets, projects, inspections, and published results they are authorized to see. Giving every audience the same internal dashboard would expose irrelevant controls and increase the chance of disclosing internal review material.

## Decision

Dashboard access requires an account and an explicit invitation created in Admin. Access is hierarchical and may be granted at business-unit, asset, project, or inspection scope. A grant includes permitted descendants and never expands beyond its tenant. Revocation takes effect immediately.

Dashboard adapts its home and navigation to role and scope:

- Authorized internal users see risk, pending work, operational status, and the actions permitted by their role.
- Clients and property owners start from their permitted assets and projects, with review timelines, progress, and published results.
- Customer-facing views omit internal notes and unpublished analysis.

Admin and Dashboard use the same identity for sign-in continuity, but entitlement to one product never implies entitlement to the other.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Internal Users Only

- **Description**: Keep Dashboard restricted to tenant staff and distribute customer reports outside the product.
- **Pros**: Simplest visibility model.
- **Cons**: Customers cannot follow review progress or retrieve their authorized history directly.
- **Why rejected**: Permissioned customer access is a central goal of the new Dashboard.

### Alternative 2: Shared View for Every Role

- **Description**: Show one universal dashboard and hide only forbidden actions.
- **Pros**: One information architecture.
- **Cons**: Customer users still encounter internal concepts and may infer hidden operational information.
- **Why rejected**: Different audiences need different starting points and disclosure boundaries, not only disabled buttons.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Customers can follow their own assets and projects without receiving tenant-wide access.
- Internal users retain operational workflows and risk-oriented triage.
- Explicit invitations and hierarchical grants provide understandable ownership.

### Negative

- The same resource may need distinct internal and customer presentations.
- Administrators must manage customer access lifecycle.

### Risks

- Hierarchical grants may be misunderstood; Admin must preview effective access before saving.
- Resource moves could accidentally change inherited access; affected access must be explained before the change becomes effective.
- Cached screens could outlive revocation; denied access must replace stale content immediately.

## Implementation Notes

The Technical Specification must map the confirmed hierarchy onto existing tenant, business-unit, asset, project, and inspection authorization while introducing only the minimum new customer membership or publication capabilities required.
