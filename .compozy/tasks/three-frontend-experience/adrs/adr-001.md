# ADR-001: Separate the Product into Three Independent Frontend Experiences

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-07

## Context

The existing Inspection web application combines tenant administration, operational review, and external evidence capture. These audiences have different goals, information density, permissions, security expectations, and usage contexts. The combined internal page makes configuration and review compete for attention, while the external capture journey must remain narrowly scoped and mobile-first.

## Decision

Provide three independent frontend products:

- **Admin** for tenant configuration, registrations, permissions, policies, and governance.
- **Dashboard** for inspection progress, operational review, reports, and permitted operational actions.
- **Capture** for invitation-scoped origin evidence, inspection evidence, and recapture.

Each product has its own entry URL, session boundary, and release lifecycle. Admin and Dashboard share sign-in continuity for users entitled to both products, but each product evaluates its own authorization. Capture remains separate from authenticated internal and customer sessions.

The three products share the Inspection brand, language, accessibility standards, and safe design primitives while using layouts suited to their audience.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Keep One Unified Application

- **Description**: Retain administration, review, and capture in one frontend with route-level separation.
- **Pros**: Fewer deployments and maximum code sharing.
- **Cons**: Continues to mix audiences, navigation, session concerns, and release priorities.
- **Why rejected**: The requested product direction requires each audience to receive a focused, independently evolvable experience.

### Alternative 2: Keep Admin and Dashboard Together

- **Description**: Separate Capture but keep all authenticated functionality in one internal product.
- **Pros**: Preserves a single authenticated shell and simpler navigation.
- **Cons**: Configuration-heavy administration remains mixed with day-to-day monitoring and customer review.
- **Why rejected**: Admin and Dashboard have sufficiently different jobs, permissions, and information architecture to justify separate products.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Each audience receives focused navigation and terminology.
- Capture remains isolated from authenticated internal and customer data.
- Admin and Dashboard can evolve and release independently.
- Shared branding preserves product cohesion without forcing identical layouts.

### Negative

- Cross-product consistency requires explicit ownership.
- Users entitled to multiple products need clear transitions and orientation.
- Independent products increase acceptance and release surfaces.

### Risks

- Shared sign-in could be mistaken for shared authorization; every product must make denied access clear and evaluate its own entitlement.
- Duplicated concepts could drift; shared vocabulary and behavioral contracts must remain canonical.
- Deep links may send users to a product they cannot access; the denial experience must preserve context and recovery guidance.

## Implementation Notes

The Technical Specification must define application boundaries, URLs, shared packages, authentication clients, deployment topology, and migration of existing routes. It must preserve the current backend business contracts unless a product requirement in this PRD requires an access or sharing extension.
