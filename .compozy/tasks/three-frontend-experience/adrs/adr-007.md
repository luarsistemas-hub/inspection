# ADR-007: Separate Product Entitlements from Roles and Resolve Scope Hierarchies at Read Time

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-07

## Context

The existing access model stores one tenant role and exact resource scopes on a
membership. It has no product entitlement, customer role, or `PROJECT` scope,
and exact scope matching does not grant access to descendants. The PRD requires
Admin-only tenant administrators, a mixed internal/customer Dashboard, explicit
business-unit/asset/project/inspection grants, explainable inheritance, and
immediate revocation.

## Decision

Keep one role per tenant membership, add `CUSTOMER_VIEWER`, and introduce
`access.product_entitlements` with values `ADMIN` and `DASHBOARD`. Tenant
administrators may receive both; customer memberships may receive only
`DASHBOARD`. Product entitlement is checked independently from role.

Continue storing only explicit grants in `access.resource_scopes`, add
`PROJECT` as a valid scope kind, and resolve effective access at request time
through canonical tenant relationships:

- business unit grants its assets, projects, and inspections;
- asset grants its projects and inspections;
- project grants its inspections;
- inspection grants only itself.

The resolver returns both the effective decision and the direct grant that
caused it. It fails closed for missing, moved, archived, inactive, or cross-
tenant relationships. Every protected action reloads current membership state,
so disabling a membership or deleting an entitlement/grant takes effect
immediately.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Encode products as roles

- **Description**: Add roles such as `ADMIN_MANAGER` and `DASHBOARD_CUSTOMER`.
- **Pros**: No new entitlement table.
- **Cons**: Creates a combinatorial role matrix and conflates job authority with product entry.
- **Why rejected**: Product access must be independently grantable and revocable.

### Alternative 2: Materialize every descendant grant

- **Description**: Expand a business-unit grant into rows for all current descendants.
- **Pros**: Simple exact-match authorization queries.
- **Cons**: Resource moves and creation require fan-out updates and create stale-access windows.
- **Why rejected**: Immediate and explainable revocation is more important than avoiding indexed hierarchy lookups.

### Alternative 3: Distinct customer and property-owner roles

- **Description**: Model the two customer personas as separate authorization roles.
- **Pros**: Allows different policies later.
- **Cons**: No current business rule differs between them.
- **Why rejected**: A single least-privilege `CUSTOMER_VIEWER` role satisfies the current scope without speculative policy.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Product entry, tenant authority, and resource visibility remain orthogonal and auditable.
- New descendants automatically inherit the intended explicit ancestor grant.
- Revocation and hierarchy changes cannot leave copied grants behind.
- Admin can explain why a resource is visible.

### Negative

- Authorization queries perform indexed relationship checks instead of a single exact comparison.
- Existing resolvers must provide canonical resource context to the authorizer.

### Risks

- **Hierarchy query cost**: add composite tenant/foreign-key indexes and measure authorization latency.
- **Moved-resource ambiguity**: access follows the current canonical hierarchy; audit history records the previous relationship and grant changes.
- **Customer privilege escalation**: database constraints and use-case validation forbid Admin entitlement or mutable roles for `CUSTOMER_VIEWER`.

## Implementation Notes

- Extend `requestctx.Principal` with verified audience and product entitlements.
- Replace boolean mutation authorization with an explicit authorization request.
- Add a tenant-scoped effective-access query for Admin previews and explanations.
- Preserve the existing resource-scope table and column names.
