# ADR-017: Design for the Confirmed MVP Capacity and Latency Envelope

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-02

## Context

Data shape, indexes, pool sizes, worker concurrency, pagination, and observability require a stated envelope. The user selected 500 concurrent capture sessions globally, 100 per tenant, 100,000 assets per tenant, and one million historical inspections per tenant.

## Decision

Design for 500 simultaneous capture sessions globally and 100 per tenant, 100,000 assets and one million historical inspections per tenant, up to 200 active photos per inspection or stage, and 20 MiB per original.

Excluding file transfer and external-provider latency, target p95 of 300 ms for simple GraphQL queries, 500 ms for mutations, and 2 seconds for dashboard aggregates. Use cursor pagination, tenant-prefixed composite indexes, bounded worker concurrency, aggregate read models, and direct object-storage upload. Treat these values as engineering capacity targets, not commercial quotas.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Small POC Envelope

- **Description**: Optimize for 100 concurrent sessions and 10,000 assets per tenant.
- **Pros**: Lower initial infrastructure requirements.
- **Cons**: Risks early redesign for large property portfolios.
- **Why rejected**: The user selected the larger operational target.

### Alternative 2: Internet-Scale Envelope

- **Description**: Design immediately for millions of assets and thousands of sessions per tenant.
- **Pros**: High theoretical ceiling.
- **Cons**: Encourages premature partitioning and operational complexity.
- **Why rejected**: It exceeds confirmed MVP needs.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Performance acceptance can be tested with concrete loads.
- Storage and query decisions have a shared capacity basis.
- Horizontal scale remains possible without premature tenant sharding.

### Negative

- Load fixtures and object-storage capacity tests are substantial.
- Pure aggregate consistency is eventual for dashboard views.

### Risks

- Large evidence histories may dominate storage; monitor bytes by tenant and retention backlog.
- Dashboard projections may lag; publish lag metrics and expose last-updated time.

## Implementation Notes

Partition tables only after measurements demonstrate a need. Preserve opaque cursor contracts so storage can evolve without changing clients.
