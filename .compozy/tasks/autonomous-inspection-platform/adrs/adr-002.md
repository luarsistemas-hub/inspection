# ADR-002: Treat Evidence Metadata as Risk Signals, Not Proof of Authenticity

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-02

## Context

Autonomous inspections depend on photos captured outside direct staff supervision. Camera origin, gallery origin, hashes, timestamps, device data, GPS, precision, and geofence distance improve traceability but cannot prove that a photographed scene is truthful. Overstating those signals would create false confidence for internal reviewers.

## Decision

Present evidence provenance as an auditable chain of custody and a set of explicit risk flags. Live camera is the default; gallery evidence remains allowed and flagged. GPS is required by default, with a template policy and asset override. Missing valid GPS blocks capture when required and becomes a flag when optional. Being outside the geofence never blocks submission and always records a visible flag. The product must not describe evidence as fraud-proof, tamper-proof, or legally conclusive.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Block Every Suspicious Capture

- **Description**: Reject gallery evidence, out-of-geofence capture, and any weak metadata.
- **Pros**: Produces a more uniform evidence set.
- **Cons**: Creates false rejections from indoor GPS, incorrect coordinates, device limitations, and legitimate gallery use.
- **Why rejected**: The user chose flags for gallery and geofence and configurable enforcement only for GPS availability.

### Alternative 2: Accept Evidence Without Provenance Labels

- **Description**: Store photos without exposing risk signals.
- **Pros**: Simpler participant and reviewer experience.
- **Cons**: Reviewers cannot distinguish capture conditions and may overtrust evidence.
- **Why rejected**: Traceability and transparent flags are central to autonomous inspection value.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Reviewers can judge evidence with the capture context visible.
- Device and location failures do not masquerade as verified provenance.
- Product claims remain proportionate to actual evidence guarantees.

### Negative

- Some submitted inspections will require human interpretation.
- Required GPS can block participants on devices that cannot provide valid location.

### Risks

- Tenants may interpret flags as proof of misconduct; mitigate with neutral language and explanatory labels.
- Excessive flags may create review fatigue; mitigate with filtering and clear severity semantics.

## Implementation Notes

The TechSpec must define how metadata is verified and which quality thresholds constitute valid GPS without changing the product semantics above.
