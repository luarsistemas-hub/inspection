# ADR-003: Support Recurring, Milestone, Manual, and Multi-Stage Inspection Lifecycles

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-09-02

## Context

Periodic property inspections are time-driven, construction inspections follow planned and exceptional milestones, and cleaning inspections can compare an origin with one or more later stages. A single scheduling model cannot express all confirmed business journeys.

## Decision

Allow inspection occurrences to be created by recurrence, a planned milestone, or an authorized manual action. A template may enable multi-stage capture for any segment, declare planned stages, and allow authorized internal users to add exceptional stages with an audited reason. Authorized users explicitly close a multi-stage project or cycle and may reopen it with an audited reason. A planned stage may be skipped with justification; its absence remains visible and causes `ATTENTION`. Existing inspections and projects retain the template version fixed when they start; newly created occurrences use the active template version.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Recurrence and Manual Creation Only

- **Description**: Model construction milestones as manual inspections.
- **Pros**: Fewer lifecycle concepts.
- **Cons**: Loses planned-stage expectations and makes construction progress harder to audit.
- **Why rejected**: The user explicitly selected milestone initiation.

### Alternative 2: Fixed Stage Count with Automatic Closure

- **Description**: Close a project after all template stages run.
- **Pros**: Predictable automatic lifecycle.
- **Cons**: Cannot represent exceptional inspections or intentionally skipped stages.
- **Why rejected**: Construction and cleaning require controlled deviations with preserved reasons.

## Consequences

### Positive

- One product supports periodic, stage-driven, and ad hoc work.
- Historical behavior remains reproducible after template changes.
- Exceptional and skipped stages remain transparent.

### Negative

- Users must understand projects, stages, occurrences, closure, and reopening.
- Multi-stage reporting has more states than a standalone inspection.

### Risks

- Stale projects may remain open; mitigate with dashboard status and due-date visibility.
- Unauthorized stage changes could undermine history; mitigate with scoped permissions and audit records.

## Implementation Notes

Technical scheduling, locking, and idempotency mechanisms belong in the TechSpec. Product behavior requires at most one occurrence per scheduled event.
