# ADR-005: Separate coverage, condition, and comparison conclusions

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-11-03

## Context

An inspected component can be visibly damaged yet well covered, or apparently sound in one view yet only partly observed. The current dashboard can claim that all expected items were checked when cataloging produced no problems.

## Decision

Present coverage, visible condition, and historical change as separate axes. Count coverage from confirmed expected components; show unknown coverage without a percentage when no expected scope exists. Require evidence from the relevant side or sides before stating normal condition, absence, or unchanged status. Use the same pinned facts, limitations, version, and audience policy in screen and PDF presentations.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: A single completion score

- **Description**: Combine fields, image count, and condition in one percentage.
- **Pros**: Simple visual summary.
- **Cons**: Obscures the denominator and confuses completion with conservation.
- **Why rejected**: It cannot explain important gaps.

### Alternative 2: Exception-only checklist

- **Description**: Show only damage findings.
- **Pros**: Short report.
- **Cons**: Hides apparently sound components and future comparison coverage.
- **Why rejected**: The descriptive checklist is the core need.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Readers can see both what was observed and what remains unknown.

### Negative

- Reports carry more structured detail than exception-only views.

### Risks

- Conflicts between catalog and findings can still occur; the report must preserve the finding and show the unresolved conflict.

## Implementation Notes

Group presentation without changing the per-component denominator. Do not rewrite issued report versions when media later expires.
